# AIDA Model

The **AIDA model** describes the four consecutive stages through which successful advertising leads a potential customer from the first contact to the purchase. The model goes back to the American advertising pioneer Elmo Lewis (1898) and is used both to design and to evaluate advertising. Advertising often addresses [latent needs](./needs-and-demand.md#types-of-needs) that the customer is not yet aware of.

## Stages

| Stage         | Goal                                                             | Typical means                                                              |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Attention** | The advertisement is noticed among competing stimuli.            | Striking images, colours or music, unusual headlines, well-known faces     |
| **Interest**  | The customer wants to know more about the product.               | Information on features and benefits, a problem the product solves         |
| **Desire**    | The customer wants to own the product.                           | Emotional appeal, comparison with the current situation, reviews, scarcity |
| **Action**    | The customer performs the intended action, usually the purchase. | Call to action, "Buy now" button, time-limited discount, simple ordering   |

The intended action does not have to be a purchase. A newsletter sign-up, a test drive or a request for an offer can be the goal of the advertisement as well.

## Example

A smartphone manufacturer advertises a new model on social media.

| Stage     | Implementation                                                                           |
| --------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Attention | An eye-catching video with catchy music stands out while scrolling through the feed.     |
| Interest  | The video shows the new camera with impressive night photos.                             |
| Desire    | Compared with the photos of their older phone, the viewer thinks "I want that".          |
| Action    | A "Buy now" button with a discount valid for 48 hours leads directly to the order.       |

## Extensions

- **AIDAS:** Adds *Satisfaction*. A satisfied customer buys again and recommends the product, which makes satisfaction after the purchase part of the advertising goal.
- **AIDCA:** Adds *Conviction* between Desire and Action. The customer is convinced by arguments such as test results or guarantees that the purchase is the right decision.

## Limitations

- Real purchase decisions are not linear. Impulse purchases skip stages, and expensive purchases involve research and comparison that the model does not cover.
- The model views the customer as passive. Today customers often search for information themselves, e.g. through reviews, comparison portals and social media.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Confusing Interest and Desire:** Interest is the wish to learn more about a product; Desire is the wish to own it. A customer who reads the technical data is interested, but does not necessarily want to buy.
