# Needs, Requirements and Demand

A **need** is the feeling of a lack combined with the desire to fulfill it. The number of needs is unlimited, while the means of satisfying them are scarce. This tension is the starting point of all economic activity and the reason for the [economic principle](./economic-principle.md).

## Types of Needs

| Criterion           | Type              | Description                                                                                      | Examples                                     |
| ------------------- | ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------- |
| Urgency             | Existential needs | Have to be satisfied to stay alive.                                                              | Food, drink, clothing, shelter               |
| Urgency             | Cultural needs    | Arise from the cultural and social standard of a society.                                        | Books, cinema, education, smartphone, travel |
| Urgency             | Luxury needs      | Go beyond the usual standard of living and can be afforded by few.                               | Sports car, yacht, designer jewellery        |
| Means of fulfilment | Material needs    | Directed at tangible goods and services and can be satisfied with money.                         | Food, clothing, a new laptop                 |
| Means of fulfilment | Immaterial needs  | Directed at intangible values and cannot be bought.                                              | Friendship, love, recognition, self-esteem   |
| Bearer              | Individual needs  | Arise in the individual and are satisfied by them.                                               | Clothing, a holiday                          |
| Bearer              | Collective needs  | Arise in the community and are satisfied by it, usually by the state.                            | Roads, schools, police, public broadband     |
| Awareness           | Conscious needs   | Are already felt by the person.                                                                  | Hunger, wish for a faster computer           |
| Awareness           | Latent needs      | Exist but are not yet felt. Advertising aims to awaken them (see [AIDA model](./aida-model.md)). | A product category the person does not know  |

The boundary between existential, cultural and luxury needs shifts with time and society. A smartphone was a luxury good in 2007 and is a cultural need today.

## Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs

The American psychologist **Abraham Maslow** described human needs in 1943 as a hierarchy of five levels. A higher level becomes the dominant motivation only once the levels below it are largely satisfied: a person who is hungry or has no home rarely strives for recognition or self-actualisation.

| Level | Need                | Description                                        | Examples                                 |
| ----- | ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| 5     | Self-actualisation  | Developing one's own abilities and potential       | Hobbies, studies, creative work          |
| 4     | Esteem              | Recognition, status, respect and self-confidence   | Praise from the manager, promotion       |
| 3     | Social needs        | Belonging, friendship, love, being part of a group | Circle of friends, family, team          |
| 2     | Safety              | Protection, order, stable living conditions        | Secure job, own flat, insurance, savings |
| 1     | Physiological needs | Basic bodily needs                                 | Food, drink, sleep, warmth               |

Levels 1 to 4 are **deficiency needs**: their absence causes discomfort, and once satisfied they no longer motivate. Level 5 is a **growth need** (in some sources *insatiable need*): it can never be satisfied completely, because self-actualisation has no end point.

Which needs matter on which level differs from person to person and with the life situation.

### Criticism

- The strict order of the levels is not proven empirically. People also pursue higher needs while lower ones remain unsatisfied, e.g. an artist living in poverty.
- The model reflects an individualistic Western culture. In collectivist cultures social needs can rank above esteem.
- Several levels usually motivate at the same time. Maslow himself described the levels as overlapping rather than strictly sequential.

## From Need to Demand

| Term            | Definition                                                                              |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Need**        | Feeling of a lack combined with the desire to remove it                                 |
| **Requirement** | The part of the needs that is backed by purchasing power and refers to a specific good  |
| **Demand**      | The part of the requirement that is brought to the market as an actual intention to buy |
| **Supply**      | The goods and services that companies offer on the market in response to the demand     |

The relationship works in both directions. Demand determines the supply a market provides: the higher the demand, the larger the supply. A large supply of a good can in turn awaken new needs, both through advertising and through imitation of others ("Everyone is buying it, so I want it too"). Usually only a small part of all needs is satisfied, because purchasing power is limited.

### Example

| Step        | Situation                                                                                                  |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Need        | A trainee sees new sneakers on a colleague and wants a pair as well.                                       |
| Requirement | The trainee has 150 EUR available and the wish refers to a specific model. The need becomes a requirement. |
| Demand      | The trainee goes to a shop and buys the sneakers. The requirement becomes demand on the market.            |
| Supply      | Many trainees buy the same model. The retailer reorders it and places it more prominently in the shop.     |

## Common Mistakes

1. **Equating need and demand:** A need alone has no effect on the market. Someone who wants a sports car but cannot pay for it has a need, not a requirement, and creates no demand.
2. **Treating the levels of the pyramid as strictly sequential:** A level does not have to be satisfied completely before the next one motivates. Partial satisfaction is sufficient, and several levels can be active at the same time.
