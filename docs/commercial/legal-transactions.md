# Legal Transactions

A **legal transaction** (German: *Rechtsgeschäft*) under the German Civil Code (BGB) consists of at least one declaration of intent and aims at a legal effect, e.g. creating, changing or ending a legal relationship. The most common legal transaction in business is the [purchase contract](./purchase-contract.md).

## Declaration of Intent

A **declaration of intent** (*Willenserklärung*) expresses a will that is directed at a legal effect. It can be made in different ways.

- **Explicit:** Orally, in writing or electronically, e.g. an order in a web shop.
- **By conclusive behaviour:** An action from which the will can be inferred, e.g. placing goods on the checkout belt, getting into a taxi or raising a hand at an auction.

A declaration that has to be received, such as a termination, becomes effective when it reaches the recipient (§ 130 BGB). Whether the recipient reads it is irrelevant. A letter in the mailbox has been received once the mailbox can be expected to be emptied under normal circumstances.

## Types of Legal Transactions

| Type                              | Declarations                     | Examples                                                |
| --------------------------------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Unilateral, requiring receipt     | One, addressed to another person | Termination, contestation, withdrawal from a contract   |
| Unilateral, not requiring receipt | One, effective without recipient | Will, public offer of a reward                          |
| Contract binding one party        | Two matching declarations        | Donation, surety                                        |
| Contract binding both parties     | Two matching declarations        | Purchase contract, rental contract, employment contract |

A **contract** always requires at least two declarations of intent that match in content: the offer and the acceptance. Whether a contract is called unilateral or bilateral refers to the obligations it creates, not to the number of declarations. A donation is a contract, because the recipient has to accept it, but only the donor is obligated.

## Capacity to Contract

**Legal capacity** (*Rechtsfähigkeit*) is the ability to hold rights and obligations and begins with birth (§ 1 BGB). The **capacity to contract** (*Geschäftsfähigkeit*) is the ability to make legally effective declarations of intent and depends mainly on age. A person with a permanent mental disorder that excludes free will has no capacity to contract regardless of age (§ 104 No. 2 BGB).

| Age     | Capacity to contract   | Consequence                                                                                  |
| ------- | ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Under 7 | None (§ 104 No. 1 BGB) | Declarations are void (§ 105 BGB). A legal representative acts on the child's behalf.        |
| 7 to 17 | Limited (§ 106 BGB)    | Contracts require the consent of the legal representatives, usually the parents (§ 107 BGB). |
| From 18 | Full                   | Declarations are effective without restriction.                                              |

A contract made by a minor without prior consent is **pending ineffective** (§ 108 BGB). It becomes effective when the legal representatives approve it and finally ineffective when they refuse. Exceptions in which a minor can act alone:

- **Solely legal advantage:** A gift without obligations, e.g. a present of money (§ 107 BGB).
- **Pocket money paragraph:** A contract fulfilled in full with means given to the minor for this purpose or for free disposal (§ 110 BGB).
- **Authorised employment:** Legal transactions relating to an employment relationship the parents have authorised (§ 113 BGB).

## Void and Voidable Legal Transactions

A **void** legal transaction (*nichtig*) is invalid from the start. Nobody has to take action for this.

| Reason                                           | Example                                                                    |
| ------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| No capacity to contract (§ 105 BGB)              | A six-year-old buys a game console.                                        |
| Sham transaction (§ 117 BGB)                     | A lower price is recorded in the contract to save taxes.                   |
| Joke transaction (§ 118 BGB)                     | "I'll sell you my car for 1 EUR" said in obvious jest.                     |
| Lack of prescribed form (§ 125 BGB)              | A plot of land is sold without notarial certification.                     |
| Violation of a statutory prohibition (§ 134 BGB) | A contract for undeclared work.                                            |
| Immorality, usury (§ 138 BGB)                    | A loan at an interest rate far above the market rate, exploiting distress. |

A **voidable** legal transaction (*anfechtbar*) is valid until one party contests it. The contestation then makes it void from the start (§ 142 BGB).

| Reason                                                  | Example                                                             | Deadline                            |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Error in content (§ 119 (1) BGB)                        | The buyer orders a "gross" of pens and assumes it means 100 pieces. | Without undue delay after discovery |
| Error in declaration (§ 119 (1) BGB)                    | A typo in the offer states 1,000 EUR instead of 10,000 EUR.         | Without undue delay after discovery |
| Error about an essential characteristic (§ 119 (2) BGB) | A painting is bought as an original but turns out to be a copy.     | Without undue delay after discovery |
| Incorrect transmission (§ 120 BGB)                      | A messenger passes on a wrong quantity.                             | Without undue delay after discovery |
| Fraudulent misrepresentation (§ 123 BGB)                | A used car is sold as accident-free despite a known accident.       | One year after discovery            |
| Unlawful threat (§ 123 BGB)                             | A contract is signed under the threat of violence.                  | One year after the threat has ended |

A party that contests because of an error has to compensate the other party for the damage it suffered by relying on the declaration (§ 122 BGB).

## Common Mistakes

1. **Treating only written contracts as binding:** A contract can generally be concluded without any particular form, including orally or by conclusive behaviour. A specific form is only required where the law or an agreement between the parties prescribes it, e.g. notarial certification for the sale of land (§ 311b BGB) or written form for terminating an employment relationship (§ 623 BGB).
2. **Silence as acceptance:** Silence does not create a contract. One exception is the commercial letter of confirmation: between merchants, silence in response to it counts as consent to its content. A consumer who receives goods without having ordered them neither has to pay nor return them (§ 241a BGB).
3. **Equating void and voidable:** A void transaction never takes effect. A voidable transaction is effective until it is contested, and without contestation it remains valid.
4. **Contesting because of an error in motive:** An error about the reason for a transaction is not a ground for contestation. A buyer who purchases shares expecting their price to rise cannot contest the purchase when the price falls. The same applies to a calculation error the other party could not recognise.
5. **Applying the pocket money paragraph to instalment purchases:** § 110 BGB requires the contract to be fulfilled in full with the minor's means. A purchase on instalments remains pending ineffective until the last instalment has been paid, unless the parents have consented beforehand or approve it.
